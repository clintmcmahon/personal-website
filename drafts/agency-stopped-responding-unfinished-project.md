WRITING PROMPT

WHAT THIS POST IS ABOUT:
Someone is in a bad situation right now. They paid an agency, the agency went
quiet, and they do not know whether they have anything of value. They are
searching at 11pm. This is the highest emotional intent post on the list and
the one most likely to produce a phone call.

The post has to be genuinely useful on its own, including the steps they should
take before hiring anyone. If it reads as a sales funnel it fails.

THE ANGLE THAT MAKES THIS YOURS:
You get called into these. You know what the wreckage looks like and what is
usually salvageable. You also know the thing they do not think to do in the
first 48 hours, which is secure access before relationships deteriorate further.

QUESTIONS TO WORK THROUGH:
1. What should they do in the first 48 hours? Repository access, domain
   registrar, hosting accounts, DNS, third-party service accounts, database
   backups. What gets lost most often?
2. How do they tell whether what they have is worth keeping without hiring
   someone? Are there signals a non-technical person can check?
3. What percentage of these, in your experience, are salvageable versus restart?
   Be honest even if the answer is unflattering to your own service.
4. What are the contract and payment questions they should get straight before
   spending more money?
5. What does a competent assessment actually look like, so they can tell whether
   the next person is doing it properly?
6. What is the most common mistake people make when they panic here?

STRUCTURE SUGGESTION:
- First 48 hours checklist. Lead with this. It is the most useful thing you can
  give them and it costs them nothing.
- How to tell what you have.
- Finish or restart, and how that call gets made.
- What to ask the next developer.
- The mistake to avoid.
- Close with the audit as one option, linking /services/rescue-recovery, but keep
  it low key. The post has already earned the call.

LENGTH: 700-750 words
AVOID:
- Any hint of enjoying their misfortune or of "this is why you hire properly."
- Bashing agencies as a category. Sometimes the client caused this.
- Making the assessment sound mandatory. Give them things they can do alone.
