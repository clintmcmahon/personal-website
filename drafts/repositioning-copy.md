### Section: The three ways this usually starts

Heading: **Where most people find me**

Intro line: Almost every engagement begins in one of three places.

**You do not know what you have.**
The original team is gone. The documentation is stale or fictional. Nobody can tell you what it would take to change anything, so nothing gets changed. Every estimate you get is a guess, and you can feel it.

**You know what you have and it scares you.**
It works, mostly. But it goes down at bad times, the fixes keep breaking other things and one person is quietly the only reason it stays up. You are paying for that risk whether or not it is on a budget line.

**You have to move and the move looks dangerous.**
End of life platform, acquisition, a vendor going away, a team turning over. The destination is clear. What is not clear is how you get there without breaking the thing your business runs on.

### Section: Service tiers

Heading: **How I Work**

**Assessment**

*Start here if you are not sure what you are dealing with.*

A fixed fee look at an existing system or a stalled project. I go through the software, the infrastructure and the history, then hand you a written report you can take into a budget meeting.

You get:
- A plain English picture of what you actually own
- The specific risks, ranked by what they would cost you
- An honest read on whether to finish, fix or replace
- A cost and timeline range for each option

Fixed price. Fixed timeline. No obligation to hire me for whatever comes next, and I have told plenty of people the right answer was to keep what they had.

**Stabilization**

*Start here if it is actively hurting.*

For systems that still run but should not be trusted. Outages at bad times, changes that break unrelated things, security nobody has looked at in years, or one person who is the only reason it stays up.

I fix the failures that carry real money behind them and make sure your operation does not depend on any single person staying. The result is a system where routine changes stop being frightening.

The outcome is that your software stops being a topic at the leadership level.

**Transition Leadership**

*Start here if you have to move something important.*

For moves where the risk is real: getting off a platform that is past end of life, replacing a vendor, handing a system from one team to another, merging systems after an acquisition.

I lead the move and I am accountable for it landing. That means the planning, the order things happen in, the dangerous parts done personally and making sure your team understands what they have when I leave.

**What this looks like in practice**

The national transplant registry publishes outcome data that patients and doctors use to decide where to seek care. The reports were static documents, expensive to produce and impossible for anyone to explore.

I built the platform that replaced them. Millions of records from federal research datasets, delivered through a public website fast enough to actually use, under federal security and accessibility requirements, and rebuilt every year without starting over.

That is the shape of most of my work. High stakes, heavily regulated, no room to be wrong, and the decisions made in the first month determine whether the thing is still serviceable in ten years.

I have done the same kind of work for state government, including a full modernization of Minnesota's official Secretary of State website while it stayed online the entire time.

### Section: FAQ

Heading: **Common Questions**

**Can you take over a project someone else started?**
Frequently. Inherited codebases and stalled projects are the core of what I do. I will look at it first and give you an honest read on whether finishing it or restarting it costs less.

**We already spent a lot on this. Is it salvageable?**
Often, yes. Sometimes not. The assessment exists to answer that question before you spend more. Money already spent should not decide what you do next, and an outside read is usually the only way to get past that.

**What if the answer is that we should start over?**
Then I will tell you, with the numbers behind it. I would rather lose the work than take you further down a path I do not believe in.

**What technologies do you work in?**
Primarily C# and .NET, including ASP.NET Core and Umbraco, with SQL Server and Azure behind it. React and plain JavaScript on the front end. Legacy .NET Framework modernization is a specialty.

**Do you work with agencies?**
Yes, white label. Umbraco and .NET migrations especially. You keep the client relationship and I do the work under your name.

**What does it cost?**
The assessment is a fixed fee that depends on how big the system is. Everything after that gets scoped from what it finds. Tell me what you are dealing with and I will give you a number.
